Internal Migration Flows in Rwanda (2010)

This page presents my flow mapping assignment created with FlowMapper. The map shows internal migration flows between administrative units in Rwanda in 2010. Flow thickness represents migration volume, with thicker lines showing larger flows.

Interpretation of Flow Patterns

Major Sending and Receiving Locations

From my map, I can clearly see that Kigali, the central region, acts as the main hub of migration. Kigali both receives and sends the largest flows, which is shown by the thickest lines, up to about 11,650 migrants, connected to it.

Several strong flows connect Kigali to nearby regions such as Ruhengeri to the north, Kibuye to the west, and Butare to the south. This shows that Kigali is highly connected to multiple parts of the country.

In contrast, a region like Cyangugu has fewer and thinner flows. This indicates that it plays a smaller role in migration and is less connected compared to Kigali.

General Direction of Flows

The flows in my map show a clear north-south pattern centered on Kigali. Strong flows connect Kigali to northern regions such as Ruhengeri and southern regions such as Butare.

This creates a pattern where movement is happening both into and out of Kigali along a north-south axis. The flows are also bidirectional, meaning people are moving both toward Kigali and away from it. This shows that migration is not one-directional, but rather involves continuous movement between Kigali and surrounding regions.

Relationship Between Flows, Nodes, and Geography

The node symbols show the location of each administrative unit, but the importance of each location is mainly shown by the number and thickness of flows connected to it.

Kigali stands out not because its node is larger, but because it has many thick flows connecting it to multiple regions. This shows that its importance comes from its strong connections, not just its position.

The basemap with place names helps me clearly see how Kigali is located near the centre of the country, which makes it easier for it to connect to regions in multiple directions. For example, it connects to northern regions like Ruhengeri and southern regions like Butare. This central position helps explain why it functions as a major hub in the migration network.

Real-World Explanation of Patterns

The pattern in my map suggests that Kigali likely has more economic opportunities, services, and infrastructure compared to other regions. This would attract people from surrounding areas such as Ruhengeri and Butare.

At the same time, people may also move out of Kigali to nearby regions, which explains the strong two-way flows. This could be due to factors like housing, family connections, or movement between urban and rural areas.

The map shows a hub-and-spoke pattern, where Kigali acts as the central hub connecting multiple regions across Rwanda.
